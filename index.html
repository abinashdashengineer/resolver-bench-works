01

The sensor is a rotating transformer

One carrier goes into the rotor. It comes back on two stator windings, scaled by sine and cosine of the shaft angle. The angle is in the amplitude, never in the wave itself.

02

Plot one against the other. You want a circle.

The angle you compute is the polar angle of the point (cos, sin) measured from the origin. So the measured point has to orbit the origin at a constant radius. Inject a fault and watch the shape break — each distortion produces its own signature in the angle error.

03

A perfect angle, arriving late, is a wrong angle

Filters, interrupts and PWM update timing all add delay. At speed, the shaft has moved on by the time you use the reading. This one costs torque silently — nothing looks broken.
