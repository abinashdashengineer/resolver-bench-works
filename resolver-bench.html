00

What this models, and what it does not

Stated up front, because a simulation you cannot bound is a simulation you cannot trust.

Modelled

  • Ideal transformer coupling with transformation ratio k, linear in sin/cos of the resolver electrical angle.
  • Amplitude modulation of a single-frequency carrier, with a lumped phase shift representing winding inductance, excitation amplifier and receive filters.
  • Static channel errors: per-channel offset, inter-channel gain mismatch, demodulation phase error, first-order eccentricity.
  • Delta-sigma quantisation noise for an ideal 2nd-order modulator with sinc3 decimation, used to derive an angle noise floor.
  • Discrete-time tracking observer at the PWM rate, type II or type III, integrated with an explicit Euler step.
  • Latency as the sum of demodulation window, filter group delay, observer lag and PWM update quantisation.

Not modelled — verify on hardware

  • Inverter common-mode injection into the resolver cabling. In practice this dominates the noise budget and no analytic model replaces a scope on the real harness.
  • Temperature drift of winding resistance, which moves the demodulation phase over an operating cycle. Calibrate hot.
  • Higher-order mechanical harmonics from winding distribution and rotor runout. Only 1st order eccentricity is included here.
  • Modulator overload, clipping and the non-idealities of a real analogue front end.
  • Multi-speed resolvers beyond a simple pole-pair scaling. Absolute position recovery is out of scope.
  • Fault reaction at vehicle level. Detection is modelled; the safe state is your system's problem.
Sign convention. Angles increase counter-clockwise. Electrical angle is the resolver angle times resolver pole pairs; motor electrical angle uses motor pole pairs. The two are independent and a mismatch is a classic commissioning error.

Demodulation, and why the phase term matters

Multiply each channel by the carrier reference and average over a whole number of carrier periods. The carrier cancels and sine and cosine remain. A plain rectifier would also strip the carrier — and destroy the sign, leaving you unable to tell 45° from 225°.

Amplitude falls as cosΔφ and the quadrature term leaks across as sinΔφ. The leakage is the damaging part: it tilts the calibration ellipse and adds a constant angle offset that looks exactly like a wrong motor alignment.

06

Initialisation is a sequence, not a function call

You cannot close the current loop until the angle is trustworthy, and the angle is not trustworthy until several conditions hold in order. Run it and watch the gates open.

Acquisition0 ms
Press run. At standstill the sequence can perform d-axis alignment; on a rotating shaft it must skip that step and take the offset from non-volatile memory.

Gate sequence

Why this order. Excitation must be stable before amplitude means anything. Amplitude must be valid before atan2 gives a usable seed. The seed must exist before the observer can lock without a long pull-in. And alignment requires a stationary rotor, so if the shaft is already turning you must trust the stored offset and verify it later against a back-EMF estimate.
